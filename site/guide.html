User guide

Using hopper

What hopper does with a job once it runs, what you see in the UI, what you can do there, and what happens next.

This page is for someone who runs hopper and gives it work. It assumes hopper is installed and takes jobs from your issues (Install guide). The words used here are the ones the UI uses.

Research reports

A job can research a question and report back, in place of doing the work. You read the report and decide.

Ask for research

  • Give the issue the label hopper:research, or
  • put a ## Research heading in the issue body (any heading level; the word alone in the text does not count), or
  • press Research on a job in the Queue that has not started yet, and confirm. The job then shows research report asked.

An issue with both a Research and a Proposal heading (or both labels) is researched first, then proposed, in one job and one session.

The report

The agent writes six parts: Question, Findings, Sources and evidence, Confidence, Open threads and Next step. A part it left out shows as Left out on the card. The report comes to the Research view. While it waits, the job holds no lane and keeps its agent session; you can park it.

What you can do

ButtonNoteThen
AcceptoptionalSee below.
Dig deeperoptionalThe job writes the next version. Tick open threads under Dig deeper on to name them; none ticked means the whole report.
Steerrequired: the new directionThe job writes the next version, going the way you said.

There is no Reject for research. Each round is a new version in the same job and session; earlier versions are kept, folded under earlier versions, and the Review trail lists every decision.

After Accept

  • A job asked for research (label, heading or the Research button) ends as finished. If it was asked for a proposal too, it writes the proposal next, in the same session.
  • A job that researched as part of a bigger task (its issue asked for research along the way) does not end. It goes back in the queue in the same session, is told the report was accepted, with your notes, and goes on with the rest of the task, or finishes if nothing more is asked.
  • A job you switched into research from a question: you pick what it does next (Phase shifts).

Review before you see it

By default a report comes straight to you. An admin can have escalation levels review it first, the same way as proposals: Research settings, at the bottom of the Research view (Proposals explains the settings).

Not built yet: research run as an orchestrated loop — a workhorse model that keeps a task list, worker subagents that do the tasks, and a frontier model that adds tasks or signs off before the report reaches you. Today the job's own agent session researches and writes each report, and the reviewer levels above are the only review before you.

Proposals

A job can write a proposal in place of the work: what it would do, and how. Reviewer levels can check it before you do.

Ask for a proposal

  • Give the issue the label hopper:proposal, or
  • put a ## Proposal heading in the issue body, or
  • press Propose on a job in the Queue that has not started yet, and confirm. The job then shows proposal asked.

The agent writes Goal, Approach, Alternatives considered, Risks, Effort and Context (what it read and relied on). While it waits the job holds no lane, keeps its session, and can be parked.

Reviewer levels and escalation

An admin sets these in Proposals settings, at the bottom of the Proposals view. They apply at once.

  • Reviewer levels: which of your escalation levels review, lowest first. Each level uses the model it has in Settings → Question gates. None (the default): a person reviews.
  • Who may accept: only a person (default), or the top reviewer level too.
  • Times the levels may send one back: 0 to 5, default 1.

Each level approves (it goes up to the next level), asks for changes (it goes back to the job with the level's notes), or escalates. A level that fails, times out or answers wrongly escalates; it never decides. Once the levels have sent it back as often as allowed, it comes to you. Every verdict and its notes stay on the review trail.

What you can do

ButtonNoteThen
AcceptoptionalSigned off: who, when, which version. The job ends as finished.
Request changesrequired: what to changeThe job writes the next version, which is reviewed from the first level again.
Rejectrequired: whyAsks you to confirm. The job ends.

You can decide at any stage, also while a reviewer level still has it. A viewer sees the card but cannot decide; an operator or admin can.

What an accepted proposal leads to

The proposal stays linked to its job and issue, and the job ends. Turning an accepted proposal into new issues or work by itself is not decided yet: you start that work yourself. The exception is a job you switched into a proposal from a question: it goes on as you picked at Accept, and a rejected proposal sends it back to its work (Phase shifts).

Where proposals and research show

Proposals and research are sections of their own, beside questions. The menu lists them in this order: Questions, Proposals, Research, Logins, Failures.

  • Each badge counts the items waiting on a person, seen or not, until they are decided. A ⇈ mark on a badge means one of them is high priority; those are listed first.
  • Below the open items: Earlier proposals or Earlier research reports, then, for an admin, the section's settings.
  • A card says where it came from when a question started it: Forked from a question of … or Its job switched from its question to write this.
  • An open item whose job ended or was cancelled is cancelled with it.
  • Webhooks get proposal.* and research.* events.

Phase shifts: research or a proposal from a question

Sometimes a job's question cannot be answered until someone looks into it. A phase shift sends that part to research or a proposal. A job's phase is work, research or proposal; when it is not work, a small tag beside the job's title says research, proposal, or fork: research for a forked one.

From a question card

Press Research this or Propose this. Give an optional note naming the aspect, choose How, then press Research or Propose:

HowWhat happens
ForkA separate job does the research or proposal. The first job waits on its question (holding no lane, with no time-out), or is parked, as the settings say. An accepted result answers the question; a rejected one opens the question again for a person. The fork has no issue of its own and takes its parent's priority.
SwitchThis job, in its own session, does the research or proposal first. At Accept you pick: it goes back to the work, with it as context (the default), it ends here, or, for research, it writes a proposal next, in the same session.

A job can suggest research first itself (a line Suggest: research — <the aspect> before its question), and an escalation level can suggest it too. The card then shows the job suggests research first with a one-click button such as Research (fork).

Settings

An admin sets these in Phase shifts, at the bottom of the Questions view:

  • The default mode of Research this and Propose this: Fork (default) or Switch.
  • While its fork runs, the job: waits on its question (default), or is parked where its executor can park.
  • Which escalation levels may shift a job themselves when they suggest it. None (default): only a person.

A shift works only on an open question whose job still waits on it, and only where the job's executor can do research and proposals; otherwise the card says why not.

The blast-radius gate and actor machines

hopper finds out what each machine can reach, rates how much damage a job there could do, and keeps jobs off risky machines unless they are allowed there. It all shows in the Blast radius panel on Machines.

Discovery

hopper runs a fixed, read-only check on each machine: the tools on its path, the versions of aws, terraform, tofu and kubectl, what each AWS identity (up to 8 profiles) may write, what each kubectl context (up to 8) may do, and where credentials come from — by variable name and file label only. It never records a secret or a file's content. It runs when a machine comes online, every Discover every, minutes (default 60), and when an operator presses Discover or Discover all. A container target cannot be discovered, and is never gated by its rating.

The rating

RatingWhen
highAdmin reach anywhere (it can change IAM users or policies, is an account's root, or may do anything in a cluster), or write reach to production.
mediumAny other write reach.
lowEverything else, also when no credentials were found.

Production means a name contains one of Prod names contain (default prod, production, prd) or the account is in Prod AWS accounts. Reach hopper could not confirm counts as a write unless an admin sets Not confirmed counts as to read-only.

Gated machines and actor machines

  • A machine is gated when it is rated at or above the Gate setting (default: machines rated high), or when it is an actor machine.
  • An actor machine is one you set aside on purpose for high-radius work. An admin adds it with Add an actor machine…, with its purpose and the rating expected. It is always gated.

When a job waits, and how it gets through

A job waits only when every machine it could use is gated for it. It says why: held at the blast-radius gate: … is rated high; only a job let through the gate runs there. It gets through when:

  • it matches a rule an admin set: Pass: labels (for example hopper:actor), Pass: repos, or Pass: priority from. All three are empty at first, so until a rule is set only a person lets a job through; or
  • an admin presses Let through on the held job in the Overview and confirms. It is placed at the next decision.

Flags

  • grew X → Y: a discovery raised the machine's rating.
  • declared X, rated Y: an actor machine does not match what you declared.
  • last discovery failed: the earlier findings are kept.

Not rated yet: Helm, GCP, Azure, Vault and database clients (their credentials show as credential sources only). Handing a job to a person on an actor machine is not built.

Jev for small decisions

Jev makes small, clear-cut decisions first, before a model or a person. It starts in shadow: it only records what it would pick, beside what was decided, so you can see whether to trust it.

The decisions

  • Answering a question with listed options: a job's question lists two or more numbered options. Jev is asked before any escalation level.
  • A failed job no rule explains: Jev picks between running it again and handing it to a person.

Shadow and active

Each decision has its own mode in Settings → Minor decisions (admin):

  • Off: Jev is not asked.
  • Shadow: record only (the default): its pick is recorded; nothing else changes.
  • Active: Jev decides when sure: a pick at or above Confidence needed (%) (default 85) is applied. For a question, the picked option is the answer, shown as answered by jev. For a failure, run again re-runs the job.

A pick that is not applied goes on as before: to the escalation levels or a person for a question, to Needs a person for a failure. Changes apply from the next decision.

Agreement

For each decision, over the last 30 days: Asked, Picked, Applied, Compared, Agreement and Overridden. A pick that was not applied is compared with what was decided later. Under Recent picks an operator can choose What it should have been and press Override: this changes nothing already done, only the comparison. When the agreement is high enough for you, set that decision to Active.

What always goes on to a model or a person

A decision whose text is about deleting, deploying or publishing, force-pushing, spending money, credentials or keys, sending a message, or changing permissions, and any decision for a job on a gated machine, is never decided by Jev. Its pick is recorded as consequential and not applied.

The key

Jev needs a TypeSafe key in hopper's environment: TYPESAFE_API_KEY for the admin user, HOPPER_USER_<ID>_TYPESAFE_API_KEY for a user added later. It is read at each call, so no restart is needed. Without it the page says Jev is off and which variable to set, and every decision is made as before.

Needs a person

A failed job that hopper stopped trying to fix by itself comes to Failures → Needs a person. It stays there until someone resolves it; age and restarts do not remove it. Each card starts with one plain sentence that says what happened, for example The job updated PR #616. #616 is merged. hopper checks the job's pull requests and its issue first, at once and then every 10 minutes. Under it is the button to press, with why; the other buttons follow, smaller. When the work is already done (a pull request of the job's is merged, or its issue is closed as completed), hopper marks the job done itself and the card leaves Needs a person.

Reasons

ReasonMeans
retries used upIt failed the same way each time it ran again. The cause is probably outside the job: a machine, a credential, a service or the repository.
job-specificThe job hit a problem of its own: its code, its tests, or the task as written.
automatic action offAutomatic handling is off for this kind of failure in Failures settings.
run again refusedhopper tried to run it again and was refused; the assessment says why.
dismissed from the queueIts locked queue entry was dismissed while the failure was open.

The folded Details shows the assessment, the known cause, the assessor's reasons and the error.

Resolve it, with a note

Write a Note: what you did, or what the job should do next. It goes to the job and to its issue. Then:

ButtonThen
ContinueThe job carries on with your note. If its agent session can be resumed, the same job goes back in the queue on its machine and resumes that session in its work tree, told the error and your note. Otherwise a new job runs, told the error, the assessment and your note.
Run againYou fixed the cause outside the job (a machine, a credential, the repository). A new job runs, told your note.
DoneThe work is done, by the job's pull request or by you. The Link to it (a pull request or commit) is filled in when the card knows it. The job ends as finished.
Won't doThe work should not be done, or it was not a real failure. The note is required. Its queue entry is dismissed.

A button hopper would refuse now is not shown; the card says not now and why (for example, a newer job of the same issue exists).

What goes to the issue

hopper posts one short comment: Resolved from Needs a person: and what was done, then your note and the link. It names no person, you included. Done sets hopper:done, Won't do sets hopper:rejected, and hopper:failed is removed. If GitHub refuses, hopper tries again at each sync; the card says whether the issue was told.

Follow the next job

A resolved card says what was done, by whom and when, and links the job that follows: Goes on as (the same job, continued) or Followed by (a new job), and in Queue →.

Teach it the cause

When the failure has no named cause yet, an admin can press Name this cause: what it was, what to do next time, and the decision (retry, held, redirected or needs a person). The assessor follows it for the next failure like it. Saved causes are under Known causes; Forget removes one.

Parking a job

Park a job to take it out of its lane for as long as you like, for example while you think about its question. Its lane is free at once. Its pane and agent stop. Its work tree, branch, agent session, machine and any open question are kept, and cleanup never removes them.

Park

Press Park (operator), then Park job, on:

  • a running job in the Overview's Lanes,
  • a job in the On a question group of the Overview's waiting list, or
  • a question card in Questions.

Some executors cannot park (the print-mode ones); such a job says cannot park and why.

While it is parked

It moves to Parked, its own place in the menu, with its open question: Questions and its count show only the questions that need an answer now. Parked lists one short row per job: its issue, its machine, how long it has been parked, the first line of its question, and whether it resumes its session or starts fresh. High-priority jobs come first. Parked's count in the menu reminds you of them. Open a row to read the whole question. Its question never expires, and you can still answer it: the answer is kept for when the job comes back. The Overview and the Queue list parked jobs too. A login it waited on fails. There is no time limit.

Auto-park

A question that waits on you for too long parks its job by itself, so the job does not keep its machine busy, for example at night. The default wait is 30 minutes. The wait starts when the question reaches you: time spent on automatic answering does not count. The row in Parked says why, for example Parked automatically: the question waited 30 min. Type your answer and press Answer: the job goes back in the queue and resumes its session, with no Pick up. A question that a risk rule or the consequential guard sent to you never parks its job. Set the wait in Settings → Auto-park (admin), one for high-priority jobs and one for the rest. 0 turns it off.

Resume

Press Pick up. The job waits for its own machine (held while that machine is offline, never started elsewhere), opens a new pane in its kept work tree and resumes its agent session there. If its question is still open, it waits on it again, and the question is back in Questions. To answer and resume in one step, open its row in Parked, type the answer and press Answer and pick up. A job with no recorded session asks first: Pick up and start fresh gives it a new session, told its task, the question and the answer. Cancel ends a parked job.

High priority and priority lanes

The labels

Label on the issueEffect
hopper:highPriority 75: high priority.
(none)Priority 50.
hopper:lowPriority 25: runs after the others.
hopper:backburnerNot a priority: the issue is not taken. A waiting job whose issue gets it is cancelled; a running one finishes.

A source can change the numbers in its settings. A label change applies at the next sync, also to a job that already runs.

What high priority does

  • A high tag on the job everywhere, and its questions, logins and Needs a person cards are listed first.
  • The Questions, Logins and Failures badges show ⇈ when one of them is high priority.
  • It runs again first after a failure.
  • Near a usage limit the lowest-priority lanes slow down first, so high-priority work is cut back last (Usage limits).
  • It gets a free priority lane first.

Priority lanes

hopper keeps its most reliable lanes for high-priority jobs. The Priority lanes panel on Machines shows each lane's rank, reliability, median start time and why it was or was not chosen; the Overview marks a priority lane with a star.

  • How they are chosen: by reliability, the share of a lane's runs over the last 14 days that ended without a lane fault, newer runs counting more. A lane needs 5 runs and an online machine to be ranked. A priority lane is replaced only by one clearly more reliable.
  • How many: Priority lanes, default 1; 0 turns them off.
  • While no high-priority job waits: a priority lane stays free (default), or it takes default jobs — a default job takes one only when no other lane on that machine is free; a low job never does.
  • By hand: an admin ticks lanes and presses Use these lanes; Choose by reliability goes back to the automatic choice.

A high-priority job never waits for a priority lane: when they are all busy it takes any free lane. No running job is stopped to make room.

Claude never waits on a key press

A job's Claude must never sit on a screen that waits for a key. hopper does two things.

It sets Claude up before it starts

With unattended on (the default, per herdr-claude instance in Settings → Plugins), hopper writes Claude's settings on a machine that has none — a fresh container, for example: first run done, a theme picked, self-update off in job panes. It never changes settings that already exist. Then it answers these screens by itself:

ScreenAnswered when
Trust this folder, and the project's outside CLAUDE.md importstrustWorkdir is on (default)
The bypass-permissions warningyolo is on (default)
The first-run theme, a detected API key, “Press Enter to continue” noticesunattended is on (default)

In a container, Claude's sign-in token (CLAUDE_CODE_OAUTH_TOKEN) now reaches every job.

Anything else is a question

A screen hopper may not answer — a login screen, Not logged in, an unknown screen, or one of the above with its option off — becomes a question. The job waits for an answer, keeps its pane, and the question climbs the escalation levels to you like any other. It shows the screen's text and options; with no sign-in it starts Claude is not signed in on this machine. Answer with an option's number or words: hopper picks it on the screen and then sends the job its task. You can also answer in the pane yourself. A screen is never a job failure.

For machines nobody watches, the executor claude (print mode) shows no screens at all; it has no pane to watch, and it always runs with every permission granted. herdr-claude stays the default.

Stale data clears itself

Open items that nothing waits on any more close by themselves, at start and on every sweep:

  • Needs a person cards close with a reason, never deleted: a newer job of its item, its job finished, its job is gone, or its item is closed (the issue was closed or deleted; hopper asks at most every 10 minutes, and a source that cannot tell leaves the card open). They leave the counts and badges; the failure history still counts them.
  • Questions whose job ended or is gone are cancelled.
  • Logins whose job no longer runs are marked failed.

Ended jobs' work trees are reaped too: every 10 minutes hopper removes an ended job's scratch folder after 24 hours, but never one that holds uncommitted or unpushed work. Failures settings has Keep cleared hand-offs (days) (default 30) and Keep records (days) (default 90).

Failures: the assessor and problems

Every failed job is assessed once, by rules (no model), and gets a decision. The Failures view shows, from the top: the counts, Needs a person, Open problems, Recent failures (the last 50, each with Retry), the failure profile, and Failures settings for an admin.

DecisionWhenThen
retryA passing cause: a daemon restart, a dropped link, a network blip, a start race.It runs again, up to 3 times, waiting 1 minute, then twice as long each time (at most 30).
held or redirectedA shared cause: a socket path too long, an expired login, a full disk, a machine offline; or one error on 3 issues within 60 minutes.A problem opens. While it is open, new jobs it covers are held, or sent to another machine. Resolve closes it; Release held lets the held jobs go.
needs a personAnything else, with what was tried and what failed.It goes to Needs a person.

A failure older than a day gets only a recommendation; one whose issue already ran again is recorded as superseded. Failures settings turn each automatic action on or off (Retry transient failures, Hold jobs for open problems, Redirect jobs to another machine), set the retries and grouping counts, and choose whether webhooks hear when a job needs a person. A failed job no rule explains can go to Jev first.

There is no separate dead-letter list: a job no automatic action can handle is a Needs a person card.

How a computer's client updates

A computer you added with Add machine runs the hopper client. hopper keeps it on its own release: when hopper updates, its computers follow by themselves.

How it works

  • Every 30 seconds hopper asks each client which release it runs. If it is not hopper's, hopper sends its release over the link, with a list of every file and its checksum. The client checks every file before it writes any, keeps the old release beside the new one, and restarts into it.
  • It never updates while a job runs there; it waits until none does. Running jobs survive the restart.
  • Machines shows the client release: (the hopper's), or (not the hopper's: loaded once no job runs there).

Older clients: the bridge

Clients from before release lists accept only a fixed set of file names. hopper sends them a small bridge release with exactly those names; it installs the real release and restarts. The computer restarts twice and needs nothing from you. If the write fails, the bridge puts the previous release back.

When a client cannot be updated

After 3 loads that do not take, hopper stops trying and the machine's card on Machines says Its client cannot be updated from here, with the reason and a line to copy. Run that line in a terminal on that computer:

curl -fsSL '<hopper URL>/client/install' | sh -s -- '<hopper URL>'

It reinstalls the client in place: the computer stays the same machine and its running jobs keep going. It needs Node.js 24 or later and herdr. Without a systemd user session (for example WSL without systemd) it says so: restart the client yourself. On a sandbox box, remove ~/.local/lib/hopper-client in the box and restart it. A client too old to report any release shows none: older than releases: add it again with Add machine. Errors from an older client now say it runs an older client release.

Not built yet: update instructions that hopper serves for each release, and a repair job hopper starts by itself when a client cannot update. Today the line on Machines, above, is the way back.

Usage limits

hopper keeps each machine inside its usage budgets. The Usage limits panel at the top of Usage shows how close you are and lets an admin move the limits.

The bands

BandUsageWhat hopper does
freebelow the soft limit (default 70%)Every lane is open.
softfrom the soft to the hard limitLanes scale down the closer usage gets to the hard limit. Lanes over the cap finish their job and take no new one, lowest priority first. No running job is stopped.
hardat or above the hard limit (default 95%)Nothing new starts. Waiting jobs say usage hard limit and how much is used.

The reading that counts is the highest of a machine's throttling budgets (the session and the week). A budget marked informational never throttles. Each budget throttles only its own agent's jobs. A job at the critical priority may take one lane past the cap, never at the hard limit, and week throttling lifts in the last hours before the week resets.

The graph

The panel shows the usage now, its band, and a graph of the last day's reading over the free, soft and hard bands, with each machine's lanes at the limits shown. An admin drags the Soft and Hard handles (or clicks the graph, or uses the arrow keys), sees the lanes each machine would have, and presses Save. Undo drops unsaved changes; Defaults goes back to the environment's values. Saved limits apply at once and win over HOPPER_SOFT_LIMIT and HOPPER_HARD_LIMIT; the panel says which applies. Others see the graph without handles.

The Overview's Usage over time graph shows each account's session and week usage as lines; the Usage view's Usage history keeps 90 days by default.
